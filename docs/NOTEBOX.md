# The note box: a found cabinet as the home-jam speaker

Notes from 2026-11-27. Nothing here is built. Every size is read off phone
photos with a tape in the shot or given by Rene from the tape; the volumes are
sums from those sizes, not measurements. Driver, amp and chip facts are from
memory of common parts: trust a data sheet over these numbers.

## What it is

A plywood cabinet found on the street: a school project by the look of it, a
dock and speaker for an iPod-sized player. Two music notes are cut through
opposite faces, a metal block with a player-sized recess sits in the top, and
a drawer runs along the bottom.

As found, inside:

- A stereo kit amp on one 8-pin chip, with screw terminals marked LEFT SP and
  RIGHT SP, a 3.5 mm socket and a clip for a 9 V block battery. The board's
  print reads like "RKAmp" and matches a kit sold to UK schools; that kit uses
  a TDA2822M from memory. The chip's marking has not been read.
- Two drivers marked 8 ohms 0.3 W, about 57 mm, one behind each note, each in
  a loose MDF plate laid over black cloth.
- Two plastic cups taped together between the plates. They hold nothing.
- A round plywood hatch on one screw, and the amp's lead through it.

## What is good and what is not

- The volume is the asset. 18 litres sealed suits an 8" woofer; the upper
  11 litres alone suit a 5.25".
- The note is a poor mouth for a driver. No circle wider than about 2.5 cm
  fits in it, so a cone pressed up behind it plays through slots. Its total
  open area is enough if the driver sits back from the panel.
- The note heads are about the size of a dome tweeter, with a clear view out.
- Two of the four walls are 2 mm skins of about 22 x 25 cm. Under a woofer
  they will ring and lose bass unless stiffened.
- The existing amp and drivers are not worth keeping in it: the amp can put
  out about three times what the drivers are rated for, and a 9 V block lasts
  an hour or two at that.

## The plan: a 2.1 box with the Daisy on top

Chosen: the whole cabinet as one sealed bass chamber.

- Drawer front glued shut and sealed, the shelf opened with large cutouts.
  About 16 to 17 litres left after the pods.
- An 8" long-throw woofer in the base, firing at the floor, the box on feet
  of 4 to 5 cm. The hole is about 18.5 cm in a 24 cm base, with a stiffening
  ring of 12 mm ply.
- Behind each note: a small mid driver on a spacer frame of 10 to 15 mm, so
  the whole note is its mouth, and a dome tweeter behind one note head. Each
  side in its own sealed pod of about half a litre, so the woofer does not
  pump the small cones.
- A 2.1 class D amp board with a sub level and crossover, about 100 W for the
  sub, on a 24 V supply of 4 A or more.
- The Seed and the 1.9" screen in the dock recess, line out into the amp.
- Weight in the bottom (a slab, a bag of sand) so the box stays put.

Fallback if only a small woofer turns up: a 5.25" on the shelf, cone down,
the upper chamber sealed (about 8 litres after pods and stiffening), the
drawer bay left open as its mouth. Bay opening about 340 cm2. The shelf hole
is about 12 cm with a ring under it. Punchy, not deep.

What to expect: with an 8" and the power, strong down to about 45 Hz, felt at
a couple of metres in a room. Not accurate, and not a party sub.

## Order of work

1. Strip it: plates, cups, amp, cloth. Keep the amp and drivers.
2. Stiffen the thin walls. The flat one: a 9 or 12 mm panel glued on inside.
   The curved one: three or four ribs of 12 mm ply sawn to the curve (trace
   it onto card from outside), glued across it about 6 cm apart, damping
   sheet between them. Rap the panels before and after.
3. Seal the dock slot and the hatch.
4. Find the woofer. What turns up picks the version above.
5. Buy the amp to match, then cut the base or the shelf.
6. Spacer frames, pods, drivers, wiring, wadding.

Steps 1 to 3 cost nothing and are the same for either version.

## Parts and where from

This is a salvage build: about 30 to 60 pounds in all.

| Part | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Woofer | 8", 4 ohms, rubber surround, for a sealed box of 15 to 20 litres | used car subwoofer, or a home cinema sub (may bring its amp) |
| Tweeters, crossover parts | dome tweeters | a charity-shop pair of bookshelf speakers (their 5" woofers are the fallback) |
| Mids | 2.5" to 3", 4 ohms, under 5 cm deep | dead computer speakers, a radio |
| Amp | 2.1 class D, sub level and crossover knobs | new |
| Supply | what the board wants, 4 A or more | an old laptop brick if the voltage suits |
| Pods, frames, ring, ribs | 12 mm ply and MDF offcuts, pipe caps | the offcut bin |

Check anything salvaged: ohms and watts on the magnet, a rubber surround (old
foam has usually crumbled), and a cone that moves without scraping.

New drivers only if the salvaged woofer runs out of travel before the amp
does, decided by ear once it plays. No spending on the note drivers: the
slots are the limit there, not the drivers.

## Open

- The chip on the kit amp, and what the amp draws: not measured.
- Whether the shelf is glued in or can come out.
- How the note slots treat the top end; whether one tweeter a side is enough.
- The curved wall: whether ribs and damping are enough or it needs a second
  skin.
- A speaker voicing on the Daisy's output (a bass lift that suits the
  chamber, a cut below what the woofer can do, a lift where the slots dull
  things). Firmware, with its own plan, after the box plays.
- Battery power. After it plays.

## Looked at the same day and parked

- **Kids' 4G watch:** screen ribbon `FPC1402415 V3`, plugs in; no data sheet
  found; the traces look like an 8-bit parallel panel. Useful parts: the
  cell, the vibration motor.
- **Green 400-in-1 handheld:** board `XXT-Q1`, processor under epoxy, a
  parallel `DG-LCD` panel. The board could carry its ten buttons for a pocket
  Daisy with the traces cut at each pad, and a 2.8" SPI panel might fit the
  window. Needs a control scheme first.
- **Akaso Brave 4:** works. A camera.
- **JBL Flip 5:** no aux socket and no way to its firmware. A wired input
  only by going round the Bluetooth chip, and only if its amp takes analogue
  audio; not opened. Over Bluetooth it is 100 to 200 ms late. Inside the box
  it would be muffled. Stays a speaker.
- **Levitation module** (ring magnet, four coils): not a speaker. Could bob a
  floating disc in the dock to the kick; its disc is lost and the base is
  tuned to it.
